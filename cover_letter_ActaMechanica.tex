\textbf{Re: Research article submission}

Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, ``Thermal expansion and voltage polarity in a BaTiO$_3$ microcantilever: A coupled plane-strain analysis'', for consideration
as a research article in \emph{Acta Mechanica}.

The paper examines how constrained anisotropic thermal expansion affects the
open-circuit voltage of a tetragonal barium titanate microcantilever under a
steady through-thickness temperature difference. Its mechanics focus is the
relationship between plane-strain compatibility, thermal loading and the polarity
of a coupled electrical response.

The principal findings and supporting analysis are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Retaining the constrained transverse thermal expansion in the full
three-dimensional thermal-stress contraction changes the predicted
piezoelectric-thermal voltage from $-0.22$~V to $+0.19$~V. The simplified reduction
underestimates the axial and through-thickness thermal-stress coefficients by
35.05\% and 57.75\%, respectively.
\item A finite-element implementation based on a single electric enthalpy and
the fixed-strain pyroelectric coefficient separates the direct pyroelectric and
strain-mediated mechanisms. The mesh-converged open-circuit voltages are
$-3.43865$~V for the pyroelectric-only case, $-1.49960$~V for the coupled case and
$+0.19321$~V for the piezoelectric-thermal case.
\item The numerical solution is checked through mesh convergence, the exact
one-dimensional pyroelectric limit, and algebraic and variational consistency
identities. Thermal-expansion sensitivity is also examined.
\end{itemize}

By connecting constitutive consistency and mechanical constraint to voltage
polarity, the study provides physical insight into anisotropic coupled-field
boundary-value problems. We believe that this thermoelastic and electromechanical
focus will be of interest to the readership of \emph{Acta Mechanica}.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. No potential conflict of interest was
reported by the authors.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
